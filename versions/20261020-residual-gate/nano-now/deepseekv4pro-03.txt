[B12] Here's the thing: "random" doesn't mean "evenly spread out." If you flip a coin 100 times, you almost certainly won't get exactly 50 heads and 50 tails — you'll get runs like HHHH or a long stretch of tails. Our brains are tuned to spot structure, so when we see a cluster, we call it a "pattern" and start thinking something must be causing it. But in a truly random process, those clumps are not only possible, they're expected — they happen all the time. It's like dropping 80 marbles into a grid: some squares get several, some get none, and that unevenness is exactly what randomness looks like. A "pattern" only becomes suspicious when it keeps showing up far more often than chance would predict across many trials — one weird-looking streak alone is just noise doing its normal thing.
