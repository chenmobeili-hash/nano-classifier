[B12] Randomness in a single trial is unpredictable, but run it thousands of times and the law of large numbers kicks in: outcomes settle into stable frequencies. A coin flip is 50/50 each time, yet after 10,000 flips you'll see roughly half heads. Simulations make this visible—individual results look messy, but the aggregate forms patterns like bell curves or uniform spreads. It's not that randomness "becomes" ordered; it's that chaos, averaged out, reveals the underlying probabilities we built into the simulation.
