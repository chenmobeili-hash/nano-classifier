  [B12] Here's the funny thing: true randomness doesn't look random to our eyes — it looks too orderly to
  us, or suspiciously clumpy. If you flip a coin 100 times, you'll almost certainly see a run of 5 or 6
  heads in a row, and it feels wrong, like the simulator cheated. But runs of that length are actually the
  expected result of pure chance. Our brains are pattern-hunting machines, tuned to find faces in clouds and
   conspiracies in dice, so when randomness happens to cluster — a streak, a "hot" number repeating — we
  read meaning into it. Real randomness is full of gaps and clumps; it's fake randomness, the kind a human
  makes up, that alternates too neatly and never repeats. So the patterns you spot in a simulation aren't
  evidence that it's malfunctioning; they're evidence that chance is messier and more textured than
  intuition gives it credit for, and that the absence of patterns would be the truly surprising outcome.
