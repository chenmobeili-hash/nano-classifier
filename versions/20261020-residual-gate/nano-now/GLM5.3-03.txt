[B07] 计划做到“完美”再开始，其实是在等一个不会到来的信号——因为完美只有在做的过程中才知道长什么样。不如反过来：先做一个“够糟但能跑”的版本，比如只做计划里最小的那一步，给它 20 分钟，做完就算今天赢了。开始之后你会发现，剩下的计划会自己变清楚。

[B08] 因为平均数只是一个“平衡点”，它不告诉你数据是怎么分布的。想象两组人的平均身高都是 1.7 米：一组人人都在 1.68 到 1.72 米之间，站在一起看起来整整齐齐；另一组一半是 1.5 米、一半是 1.9 米，站在一起高矮分明。数字的中心一样，但“形状”完全不同——一组挤在一起，另一组摊得很开。而且平均数还容易被个别极端值拉动：九个人月入五千，第十个人月入五百万，“平均”就成了五十多万，可这数字谁也代表不了。所以除了平均数，还要看数据的分散程度和有没有异常值，印象才会接近真相。

[B12] Here's the thing about randomness: it's lumpy. Our brains expect "random" to mean "evenly spread out" — like if you flip a coin 100 times, you'd guess the heads and tails alternate nicely, HTHTHT and so on. But real randomness doesn't work that way. Each flip has no memory, so nothing stops the coin from landing heads five times in a row. In fact, if you flip a coin 100 times, you should *expect* runs like that — streaks and clusters are a natural part of randomness, not evidence of a pattern behind it.

So when a simulation of random events produces something that looks like a pattern — a cluster of wins, a stretch of "lucky" rolls, a shape that seems meaningful — that's usually just randomness doing its thing. With enough attempts, even unlikely-looking arrangements become near-certain somewhere. It's like how the stars are scattered at random, yet we see bears and hunters in the constellations: the sky didn't arrange itself into shapes, we're the ones who are extremely good at finding shapes.

That skill — pattern-finding — evolved because spotting a tiger in the grass was worth the occasional false alarm. The trade-off is that we also spot "patterns" in noise. So the interesting question when you see a pattern isn't "wow, what caused this?" but "how often would something like this happen by chance alone?" Run the simulation a thousand times, and you'll often find your surprising pattern shows up regularly in pure noise. That's the tell.
