平均数只是把所有数值加起来再平分，它只说“总量摊平后每人分到多少”，完全不提这些数值彼此差多远。比如两组人收入平均都是 5000：一组人人都是 5000，另一组是四个人各 1000、一个人 21000。平均数一模一样，可第二组里多数人过得很紧，只有一个人很宽裕——你直接看这组数字，感受完全不同。所以看数据除了平均数，还要看“分散程度”（最大值最小值、极差、标准差之类），否则平均数会掩盖内部的巨大差异。

Here's the thing: "random" doesn't mean "no pattern" — it means no pattern was put there on purpose. When you flip a coin 80 times, every single sequence of heads and tails is equally unlikely, including HTHTHTHT… and HHHHHHHH. So when a run of six heads shows up, your brain says "that can't be random!" — but that run had exactly the same tiny chance as any other arrangement you'd never notice. What randomness does guarantee is the overall balance: over many flips you get close to half heads, half tails. What it doesn't guarantee is local smoothness. Clumps, streaks and clusters are not evidence of cheating; they're what unbiased chance looks like up close. The trick is that we compare what we see against how many different patterns could have looked "notable" — and there are loads of them, so some show up. A simulation shows patterns for the same reason real randomness does: we're pattern-hungry animals staring at a very large number of equally ordinary outcomes.
