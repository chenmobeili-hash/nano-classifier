343 = 13×26 + 5，所以余数为 5。

[A10]
**先手必胜**。唯一能保证获胜的第一步是**取 1 枚**。

从 0 开始逐个判断每个剩余数是必败局面（P）还是必胜局面（N）：0 为 P，1 为 N，2 为 P（只能走到 1），3、4、5、6 为 N（分别能走到 0、0、2、2），7 为 P（只能走到 6、4、3，都是 N），8 为 N，9 为 P。由此得出规律：剩余数除以 7 余 0 或 2 时是必败局面。

15 枚时的三种取法：
- 取 1：剩 14，14 除以 7 余 0，是必败局面，留给对手，先手获胜。
- 取 3：剩 12，12 除以 7 余 5，是必胜局面，对手获胜。
- 取 4：剩 11，11 除以 7 余 4，是必胜局面，对手获胜。

打个比方：两个班数学考试的平均分都是 70 分。A 班每个人都考了 68 到 72 分，大家水平差不多；B 班一半人考 100 分，另一半考 40 分。平均数一样，但你对这两个班的感觉会完全不同。A 班很"整齐"，B 班两极分化严重。

平均数只告诉你"中间大概在哪"，不告诉你数据有多分散、有没有极端值、是不是分成了几群。比如一个小区里九个普通人加一位亿万富翁，平均收入看起来很高，但这个数字代表不了大多数人。所以看数据时，除了平均数，还应该问问：大部分数落在什么范围？有没有特别大或特别小的数？最好把数据画出来看一眼形状。

Here's the weird thing about randomness: it's *lumpy*. Most people imagine random means "evenly mixed," like HTHTHTHT. But real randomness clumps. Flip a fair coin 100 times and you'll almost certainly get a run of five or six heads in a row somewhere. That's not the coin being broken. Streaks are just what randomness normally looks like.

So when a computer simulates randomness, it will also produce streaks, clusters, and things that look like shapes. Scatter random dots on a screen and you'll see little crowds and empty patches. Your brain evolved to spot patterns, like faces in shadows or tigers in tall grass, so it jumps on those clumps and shouts "that means something!" Usually it doesn't.

There's a second, sneakier reason. Computers can't actually be random. They use *pseudo*-random number generators: math formulas that take a starting number (the "seed") and churn out a sequence that looks random. Good generators are extremely hard to tell apart from real chance. Bad ones have hidden regularities. Old generators sometimes produced numbers that, plotted in 3D, fell neatly onto a few flat planes, which is a real pattern baked into the formula. And with the same seed you get the exact same "random" sequence every time, which is useful for debugging and a little eerie.

So patterns in a randomness simulation come from three places: true randomness is naturally clumpy, your brain over-reads the clumps, and occasionally the generator really does have a flaw. The fun part is figuring out which one you're looking at. Statisticians have tests for exactly that.
