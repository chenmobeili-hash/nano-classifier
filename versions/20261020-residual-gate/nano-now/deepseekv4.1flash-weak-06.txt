[B08] 平均数只告诉你“重心在哪”，不告诉你数据怎么分布。比如两组人身高平均都是170厘米：一组几乎全是168到172厘米的人，你会觉得“大家差不多”；另一组是150和190两个极端各占一半，平均同样是170，可你看到的是“高矮两极分化”。同样地，平均值一样也可能是一组很集中、一组很分散，或者一组中位数高、一组被少数极端值拉高。所以印象取决于分散程度（方差/标准差）、极值和分布形状，而不只是那一个平均数。

[B12] Because "random" describes how something is made, not what it looks like afterwards. If you flip a fair coin 20 times, every exact sequence is equally unlikely — HHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH is just as improbable as HTHHTTHTHHTHTTHTTHHT. But your brain doesn't compare exact sequences. It compares simple patterns: "all heads" jumps out, while a messy mix looks meaningless. So a streak feels special even though the specific streak is no rarer than any other lineup. The same thing happens in simulations: a random generator can churn out tens of thousands of digits and, purely by chance, produce a run of six 7s or a tidy-looking cluster. With enough draws, some remarkable-looking coincidence is almost guaranteed — that's the law of truly large numbers, not a broken simulator. To check for real bias you don't eyeball the pattern; you look at long-run frequencies and run the same experiment many times, because the pattern you noticed is probably the rarest thing you searched for, not the thing that was actually most likely.
