先手必胜。必败位置（轮到谁谁输）为 0、2、7、9、14……
推导：1、3、4 可直接取完，所以是必胜位；2 只能取 1 到达 1（对手必胜），所以 2 是必败位。5、6 可走到 2，所以必胜。7 只能走到 6、4、3（都是必胜位），所以 7 必败。8 走到 7，必胜。9 只能走到 8、6、5，所以 9 必败。10 走到 9，必胜。11、12 分别经 4 或 3 走到 7、9，必胜。13 取 4 走到 9，必胜。14 只能走到 13、11、10，所以 14 必败。
15 取 1 枚得 14（必败位），取 3 得 12、取 4 得 11 都是对手必胜位。所以唯一能保证获胜的第一步是**取 1 枚**。

平均数只告诉你"中间大概在哪"，却不告诉你数据是紧挨着中间，还是散得很开。
比如两个班的平均成绩都是 70 分。A 班几乎每个人都在 68 到 72 分之间；B 班则一半人接近满分，另一半人刚好及格甚至不及格。数字一样，但在 A 班，老师可以用同一套教法照顾所有人；在 B 班，同学之间的差距很大，需要区别对待。
再比如，一条河的平均水深是 1 米，听起来很安全，可河里可能既有只到脚踝的浅滩，也有 3 米深的深潭。所以只看平均数，容易忽略数据的"分散程度"和"极端值"，而这些往往才是人们真正有感觉的地方。

Great question! The trick is that a simulation of randomness isn't actually random, and even truly random things contain patterns.

First, computers use formulas called pseudorandom number generators. They start from a "seed" number and apply math to produce a long string of numbers that *look* unpredictable. If you start from the same seed, you get exactly the same sequence every time. So any pattern it produces is partly the result of a fixed set of rules, and a poorly designed generator can even have visible repeating patterns.

But the bigger reason is that real randomness is lumpier than our intuition expects. If you flip a fair coin 20 times, you'll very likely see a run of four or five heads in a row somewhere. That doesn't mean the coin is broken. It just means that when you generate lots of random outcomes, some streaks and clusters are bound to show up by chance. Our brains are pattern-detecting machines, so we notice those streaks and think "that can't be random," when truly random data would often look *less* even than we'd guess.

So patterns can show up in two ways: because the simulation has hidden structure, or just because chance naturally makes clumps. The tough part is telling which one you're looking at, and statisticians use tests to check whether a pattern is more than you'd expect by luck.
