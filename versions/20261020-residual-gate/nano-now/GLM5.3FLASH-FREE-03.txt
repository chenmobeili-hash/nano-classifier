[B12] Here's the funny secret about randomness: it loves making patterns, even though it isn't trying to. Imagine flipping a fair coin twenty times. Somewhere in there, you'll almost certainly get four or five heads in a row, and your brain will shout "this coin is rigged!" But mathematically, a long run is actually expected — with enough flips, streaks of heads are as inevitable as puddles after rain. Our pattern-hungry brains are the real culprit. Evolution trained us to find faces in clouds and meaning in coincidences, because noticing patterns kept our ancestors alive. That superpower now misfires on dice and shuffled songs, making us see conspiracies in pure noise. So when a simulation "shows a pattern," the right question isn't "why is it patterned?" but "is this pattern more than randomness would produce by accident?" Flip those coins long enough and you'll see every shape — hearts, zigzags, desert-island streaks — all from a coin that has no memory and no opinions. Randomness isn't the absence of shapes; it's the presence of all of them, sprinkled unevenly.
