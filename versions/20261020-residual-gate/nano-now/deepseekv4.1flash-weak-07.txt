平均数只告诉你“整体的重心在哪”，不告诉你“大家离重心多远、怎么排布”。比如两组人平均身高都是170：一组全是168到172，看起来整齐一致；另一组一半是155、一半是185，同样是170，但你站在里面会觉得高矮差得离谱。再比如平均分都是75：一组人人75上下，另一组多数90、少数30，观感完全不同。另外平均数很怕极端值：一个年薪千万的人能把整组“平均”拉高，让人误以为大家都很富。所以看数据时，还要看波动（极差、标准差）、分布形状（是否对称、有没有双峰）和个别极端值。

Here's the thing: randomness doesn't mean "no pattern" — it means "no pattern that lets you predict the next step." A pattern showing up is not evidence that the process is rigged. Flip a coin 100 times and you will very likely see runs of four or five heads in a row. That feels special, but it's just arithmetic: with 100 flips there are about 97 places a run of four could start, so even a small chance per spot adds up fast.

Same with dice and lotteries. If you roll a die 60 times, getting each number exactly 10 times is actually pretty unlikely — the counts wobble around 10 by a few, and that wobble is the normal behavior, not a bug. What randomness forbids is a *reliable* pattern, like "after two heads, tails is due." The coin has no memory; the probability is still 1/2.

The trap is that humans are amazing at finding patterns and terrible at judging how surprising they are. You notice the one streak and forget the 99 boring stretches. So watch for clusters, streaks, and coincidences — but ask "how many chances were there for this to happen?" before deciding the universe is sending a message. Usually the honest answer is: with enough chances, something like this was almost guaranteed. That's not mystery, that's just how many tries you get.
