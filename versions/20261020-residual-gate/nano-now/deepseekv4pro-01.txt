平均数只是把所有数值加起来一除，它只告诉你「中心在哪里」，却把「数据散成什么样」全部抹平了。比如甲组是 50、50，乙组是 0、100，平均数都是 50，但甲组人人一个样，乙组有人一无所有、有人全拿。所以同样的平均数，背后可能是很均匀的一群人，也可能是差距极大的一群人，给人的印象自然完全不同。

It's actually the reverse of what people expect: if randomness never produced any patterns, that would be suspicious. Think of flipping a coin a hundred times. Every single specific sequence of heads and tails is exactly as likely as any other — getting "all heads" is no more miraculous than the messy sequence you actually got. We only *notice* the tidy-looking ones: a run of six heads in a row, or your shuffle playing the same two songs back to back. Our brains are pattern-hunting machines, so out of the huge number of equally-likely outcomes, a few will always look "special" to us. Coincidences feel meaningful precisely because we're comparing them against the patterns we care about, not against all the equally-unremarkable outcomes we ignore. So when a "random" thing shows a pattern, it's usually not the randomness being broken — it's you catching one of the many accidental arrangements that were bound to show up eventually.
