[B08] 因为平均数只告诉你“重心在哪”，没告诉你数据是怎么分布的。比如两组成绩平均都是 70：一组大家基本都在 65 到 75 之间，很整齐；另一组一半人考 100、一半人考 40，同样平均 70，但体验完全不同。后者差异大、不稳定，还可能有极端值把平均数拉偏（比如一个人考了 0 分）。所以看数据时还要看分散程度（方差、极差）、中位数和有没有异常值，光看平均数容易被骗。

[B12] Because a simulation isn't actually random—it's a recipe. The computer follows rules, and rules have structure. When you roll a real die, tiny, unknowable details decide the outcome; when a program simulates it, a formula decides. That formula is designed so the results look unpredictable in the short run, but the formula itself repeats and spreads outcomes evenly over time. So two things happen: first, rare things are guaranteed to happen eventually—if you flip a coin enough, long streaks of heads must appear, and they look suspicious even though they're normal; second, randomness loves clusters. Truly random points don't space themselves neatly; they bunch up, leaving gaps, which our brains read as "patterns." Your brain is a pattern detector tuned for survival, so it spots shapes in noise. The simulation isn't hiding order—it's just that orderlessness, in large doses, naturally produces arrangements that look meaningful.
