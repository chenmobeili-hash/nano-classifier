先手必胜，且保证获胜的第一步取法有两种：取 1 枚或取 3 枚。
理由：先计算必败点（轮到谁取谁就输的剩余石子数）。记 P 为必败、N 为必胜：0 是 P；2 是 P（只能取 1，留下 1 给对方）；3、4 可一次取完，是 N；5、6 可留下 2，是 N；7 的所有取法留下 6、4、3（全为 N），故 7 是 P；同理 9 是 P（留下 8、6、5 全 N），12 是 P（留下 11、9、8 全 N），14 是 P（留下 13、11、10 全 N）。面对 15 枚时，取 1 枚留下 14（P 点），或取 3 枚留下 12（P 点），都把必败点丢给对方；而取 4 枚留下 11（N 点）不行。所以获胜第一步为：取 1 枚或取 3 枚，之后每轮都把剩余石子数保持在必败点集合 {2, 7, 9, 12, 14, …} 中。

因为平均数只概括了数据的“中心”，却完全丢掉了数据的“分散程度”和分布形状。举个例子：两组人平均工资都是 8000 元，第一组可能是 7500、8000、8500，大家彼此差不多；第二组可能是 2000、3000、19000，虽然平均一样，但绝大多数人挣得很少，只有个别人挣得特别多。看平均数会觉得两组“一样好”，可生活在里面感受完全不同。此外，平均数还容易被极端值拉偏，也可能掩盖“多数人在一边、少数人在另一边”的偏斜分布。所以单看平均数会丢掉波动、极端值和分布形态这些关键信息，印象自然可能截然不同。

That's a great question, and it touches on something that surprises almost everyone the first time they see it. When we simulate randomness—say, by flipping a virtual coin thousands of times—we expect the results to look "smooth" and evenly mixed. But true randomness doesn't work that way. In a short run, streaks are not just possible; they're guaranteed. If you flip a coin 100 times, seeing six heads in a row has a pretty good chance of happening somewhere in the sequence, even though each individual flip is fair. Our brains are pattern-seeking machines, evolved to find faces in clouds and connections in coincidences, so when we look at random data, we naturally "see" clusters, trends, and hot streaks that aren't really there as designed features—they're just inevitable artifacts of chance. Statisticians sometimes call this the "clustering illusion." The key insight is that randomness is not the same as alternation or balance. A perfectly alternating sequence like HTHTHT actually looks suspiciously regular and would be a red flag in a real simulation. So when a simulation of randomness produces patterns, it's not malfunctioning—it's doing exactly what randomness does: producing clumps, gaps, and streaks that our pattern-hungry minds then interpret as meaning something.
