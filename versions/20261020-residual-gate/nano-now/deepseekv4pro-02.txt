平均数只是把一堆数“抹平”后的一个数，它丢掉了这些数是怎么分布的。比如两组都是 5 个人、平均工资一样，一组每个人都是五万，另一组一个人五十万、另外四人几乎没钱——平均数一样，但给人的印象天差地别。所以光看平均数，就像只看一道菜的平均温度，完全不知道是均匀热乎，还是一半烫嘴一半冰凉。

It feels strange that a "random" thing can make patterns, but here's the secret: random doesn't mean "evenly spread out," it means "every outcome is equally likely each time." Flip a coin four times and HHHH is just as likely as HTHT. We just notice the streaks because our brains are pattern-hunters — we're built to spot order even where none was planted. Toss a coin a hundred times and you're almost guaranteed to see a run of five or six heads in a row, not because something broke, but because with that many chances, unlikely-looking runs become inevitable. So patterns aren't proof of hidden order; they're what randomness actually looks like when you watch it closely enough.
