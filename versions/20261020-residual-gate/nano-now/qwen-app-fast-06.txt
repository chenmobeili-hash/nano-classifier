[B12] Because randomness isn't the same as "evenly spread." Each coin flip or dice roll is independent, but when you collect many of them, some outcomes naturally cluster together just by chance. Think of it like raindrops on a sidewalk: each drop lands randomly, yet you'll still see little puddles forming here and there. Our brains are pattern-seeking machines, so we notice those clusters and think, "That looks meaningful!" But most of the time, it's just probability doing its thing. True randomness doesn't look perfectly uniform—it looks messy, with streaks and gaps that feel surprising but are actually expected.
