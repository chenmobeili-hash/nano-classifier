[B12] Here's the thing: randomness is about how each result is produced, not about the result looking messy. If you flip a fair coin 80 times, every exact sequence has the same tiny probability, but there are far more sequences with a mix of heads and tails than sequences that alternate perfectly. So streaks like HHHHH happen — not because the coin "wants" a pattern, but because there are many ways for clusters to appear. Our brains are pattern detectors, so we notice the streak and ignore the ordinary flips around it. A simulation can also show patterns if it uses a fixed seed, a weak random number generator, or too few trials. Short runs look lumpy; patterns become more stable only over many repetitions. Seeing a pattern in a small sample is often just a reminder that "random" means unpredictable in detail, not uniform at every moment.
