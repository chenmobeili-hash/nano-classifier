[B07] 我懂那种感觉——脑子里已经有一张完美的蓝图，可一想到动手就发现处处都不够好，于是干脆不动。但完美其实不是起点，它是反复修改出来的结果。要不我们把标准先降到“丑但完整”：今天只做最小的那一步，比如只写下第一句话、只画一个草图、只填表格的第一栏，做完就收工，不许回头改。等你明天再看它，你会发现自己比想象中更知道该怎么接着做。先有一个能改的版本，才有变完美的机会。

[B08] 平均数只是把所有数据“摊平”后的一个数，它不告诉你数据是怎么分布的。比如两组人体重数据平均数都是 60 公斤：A 组每个人都接近 60（58、59、60、61、62），你会觉得这是一群体型差不多的人；B 组是 40、45、55、70、90，平均数也是 60，可你会觉得这群人高矮胖瘦差别巨大。平均数相同的两组数据，可能一组很整齐、另一组两极分化，也可能一组数据都挤在中间、另一组被一两个极端值拉高了平均。所以看数据时，除了平均数，还要看离得有多散（方差或标准差）、中位数、最大最小值。就像两个人平均分数一样，一个是每科都中等，一个是几科满分几科不及格，印象当然完全不同。

[B12] Imagine you ask a computer to roll a die a thousand times. It doesn't have a hand, so it can't really toss anything — instead it runs a recipe: take a starting number, stir it with some arithmetic, and use the result to pick 1 through 6. Same starting number, same recipe, same rolls, every time. That's why a "random" simulation isn't random in the deep sense; it's a very long, complicated pattern that's hard to guess without knowing the recipe. Patterns show up for two more reasons. First, our brains are pattern-hunting machines: if you flip a fair coin 20 times, a run like HHHHH is just as likely as HTHTH, but the streak feels meaningful, so we notice it and forget the boring stretches. Second, real randomness is lumpy by nature. Pure chance doesn't spread things out evenly — it clumps. If you scatter 100 dots truly at random on a page, you'll see clusters and empty patches, and if you try to make it "look random" by spacing dots evenly, that's actually the least random thing you could do. So when a simulation produces a suspicious-looking streak, ask two questions: is the generator using a fixed hidden recipe, and am I counting the streak but not the thousands of ordinary results around it? Usually the answer is yes to both.
