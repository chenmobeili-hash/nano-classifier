**26 个。**
计数方法：首位与末位不同，分两类且对称。
类 A（首位 1、末位 0）：中间 6 位含 3 个 1，要求自身无连续三个 1，且不得同时占第 2、3 位（否则与首位凑成三连 1）。从 C(6,3)=20 个三元子集中先删掉含 {1,2} 的 4 个，再从剩下 16 个中删掉连续三元组 {2,3,4}、{3,4,5}、{4,5,6} → 13 个。
类 B（首位 0、末位 1）：镜像同理，删掉同时占第 6、7 位（即子集中的 {5,6}）的 4 个，再删连续三元组 {1,2,3}、{2,3,4}、{3,4,5} → 13 个。
合计 13 + 13 = 26。

我懂那种感觉——计划越写越漂亮，就是迟迟不开始，因为一旦动手就可能发现它不完美。其实「完美」是个会移动的目标，你只有在做的过程中才知道什么才算好。
给你一个很轻的起步办法：把目标缩到「不可能失败」的大小。比如明天要交的报告，第一步不是「写完」，而是「打开文档，写下三行最丑的草稿」，就三行，写完允许自己关掉。你会发现开头那一下最难，之后惯性会接手。
另外给整个过程定个「粗糙版交付时间」：先做出一版能看的 60 分东西，再去打磨。打磨是在已有作品上改，凭空追求完美则没有抓手，只会越来越慌。
今晚就试那三行，行不行？写完发我看看，我陪你往下推一步。

[B08]
平均数只告诉你「重心在哪」，不告诉你「数据散得多开」。
举个生活里的例子：两条路的平均通勤时间都是 30 分钟。一条路每天都稳稳 28～32 分钟；另一条路有时 10 分钟、有时 60 分钟。平均数一样，但体验完全不同——你要卡点赶飞机，肯定选第一条。
差别在于「分散程度」：数据挤在一起还是拉得很开；还在于「形状」：是大多数人都差不多、只有少数人极端（平均数被几个极端值拽走），还是整体都偏高偏低。人的感受往往由最差的那几次、由波动本身决定，而不是由那个被抹平的中间值决定。
所以只看平均数容易上当，最好再问一句：最高最低是多少？大家都差不多，还是差得很远？

Here's the thing that trips everyone up: randomness doesn't mean "evenly spread out," it means "each step is decided independently." Those are very different ideas, and patterns are what live in the gap between them.
Roll a die twenty times and you'll probably see 3, 3, 3 in a row somewhere. That feels suspicious — like the die has a favorite number. But each 3 had the same 1-in-6 chance whether or not the last roll was also a 3. The die has no memory, so a streak costs nothing to produce. What's actually rare is a *specific* sequence of twenty rolls; there are just mind-bogglingly many sequences, so *some* of them look like streaks, and the ones you'd call "random-looking" are only a tiny slice of all possible outcomes. Your brain is pattern-detecting machinery, so it spots the slice that looks meaningful and ignores the vast majority that don't.
There's also clustering. Drop raisins randomly into a cake and you get clumps and empty patches, not an even grid. Even spacing would require each raisin to know where the others are — that's a rule, not randomness. Random placement naturally produces gaps and knots; coin-flip walks wander far from the center for exactly this reason.
And the law of large numbers isn't a debt collector. It doesn't say heads must "catch up" after a run of tails. It says that as flips pile up, the *fraction* of heads settles toward one half — the early imbalance just gets diluted by sheer volume, not corrected. That distinction is the whole reason "I'm due for a win" is a trap.
So: patterns aren't bugs in a random simulation, they're signatures of it. A generated list that *never* clusters, with perfectly spaced digits and alternating coin flips, would be the fake — that's what people invent when they try to *act* random. Real randomness is lumpier than our intuition expects, and once you know that, streaks stop looking like messages and start looking like what they are: just noise, being noise honestly.
