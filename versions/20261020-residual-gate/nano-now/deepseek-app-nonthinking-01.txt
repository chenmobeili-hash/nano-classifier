[B12] Imagine rolling a die a hundred times and writing down the results. You'd expect each number about equally often, but you wouldn't expect a perfect alternating pattern like 1,2,3,4,5,6 repeating. Real randomness is lumpy: streaks happen, clusters happen. If a simulation produces a run of five heads, that doesn't mean it's broken—it can be exactly what random looks like. Patterns in a simulation are often just the brain spotting order in noise, or the result of a fixed rule (like a pseudo-random generator) that eventually repeats. True randomness doesn't guarantee "looking mixed"; it guarantees unpredictability, and unpredictability often includes clumps and streaks.
