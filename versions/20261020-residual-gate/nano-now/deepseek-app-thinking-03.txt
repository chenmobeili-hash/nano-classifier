[B12]Because “random” doesn’t mean “evenly mixed.” In a short simulation, clumps are normal: you might get several heads in a row or two 6s close together. Those clumps can look like a pattern, but they’re often just chance fluctuations. Also, computer simulations usually use pseudorandom generators—deterministic recipes designed to imitate randomness—so they can have subtle structure if you know how they work. Seeing a pattern in a limited sample doesn’t automatically prove the process wasn’t random; it may just be normal randomness or a quirk of the generator.
