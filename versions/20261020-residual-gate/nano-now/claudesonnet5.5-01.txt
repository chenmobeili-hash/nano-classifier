- 2 只能取到 1，所以是必败位置。
- 3 和 4 能取到 0，所以是必胜位置。
- 5 和 6 能取到 2，所以是必胜位置。
- 7 能取到 6、4、3，都是必胜位置，所以 7 是必败位置。
- 8 能取到 7，所以是必胜位置。
- 9 能取到 8、6、5，都是必胜位置，所以 9 是必败位置。
- 10 能取到 9，所以是必胜位置。

- 12 能取到 9，所以是必胜位置。

- 14 能取到 13、11、10，都是必胜位置，所以 14 是必败位置。

平均数只告诉你“中间大概在哪”，没告诉你数据是挤在一起，还是散得很开。
比如两个班的平均分都是 70。甲班几乎人人都在 68 到 72 分之间，说明大家水平很接近。乙班有人考 100，有人考 40，差距很大。
两个班的平均分一样，但给人的印象完全不同：甲班稳定，乙班两极分化。所以看数据时，最好再看一看最高值、最低值和波动大小，不要只看平均数。

When a computer "simulates" randomness, it usually isn't flipping a coin. It runs a formula that produces numbers that look unpredictable. Even so, patterns can show up, for two different reasons.

The first reason is that real randomness is lumpier than we expect. If you flip a fair coin 20 times, getting five heads in a row somewhere is not weird. Our brains expect randomness to look evenly mixed, like HTHTHTTH, so streaks and clusters look suspicious. In truly random data, though, streaks and clusters are normal. A random sequence that never has them would actually look fake.

The second reason is that the simulation isn't truly random. A typical generator is a deterministic recipe. Start with the same "seed" number and you get exactly the same sequence every time. Good recipes make this hard to notice, but a poorly designed one can have hidden structure, such as repeating cycles or numbers that fall into neat lines when you plot them.

So if you see a pattern, ask two questions. Is it just normal random clumping? Or is it a flaw in how the numbers were made? Often it's the first. Sometimes it's the second, and that's how people discovered bad random number generators.
