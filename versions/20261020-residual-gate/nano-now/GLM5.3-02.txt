[A10]
先手必胜，且唯一能保证获胜的第一步是取 1 枚。
理由（递推必败态）：设面对 n 枚石子的一方，0, 2, 7, 9, 14 为必败态（即模 7 余 0 或 2 的数）。验证：从这些数出发，减 1、3、4 后余数只会变成 6,4,3 或 1,6,5，均不是 0 或 2，即任何一步都把必败态送给对手；而从其余余数出发总有一步回到必败态（余1减1、余3减3、余4减4、余5减3、余6减4）。15−1=14 是必败态；15−3=12、15−4=11 都是必胜态。所以先手只能取 1 枚才能把对手留在必败态。

可以这样想：平均数只是一个“重心”，它告诉你这组数据大概的中心在哪，但完全不告诉你大家离这个中心有多散。比如两个班数学平均分都是 70 分：一个班所有人都在 65 到 75 之间，你会觉得“这个班水平挺整齐”；另一个班一半人考 95、一半人考 45，平均也是 70，但感觉完全是另一个故事——有学霸也有跟不上的人。同样的平均数，背后可以是“人人都差不多”，也可以是“两极分化”，给人留下的印象天差地别。所以看数据时，除了平均数，还要看分散程度（每个人离平均数有多远）甚至整体分布，才能知道它到底讲了个什么故事。

Here's the weird thing about randomness: it's not "smooth." People imagine randomness as even static — everything nicely mixed — but real randomness is lumpy. Flip a coin 100 times and you'll almost certainly see a streak of six or seven heads in a row somewhere. That feels like a pattern, like the coin "got hot," but it's just what happens when you give chaos enough room. In 100 flips there are about 95 different spots where a streak could start, and each of those small chances adds up. Rare things become near-certain when you get enough attempts.

There's a second layer too: you, the observer. Your brain is a pattern-finding machine — it's why we see faces in clouds and hear hidden messages in static. So when a random streak shows up, your brain lights up and says "that means something!" But you're only noticing the streak *after* it happened. You didn't predict it; you noticed it. Hindsight turns noise into what looks like signal.

Want a fun one? In a room of just 23 people, there's about a 50% chance two share a birthday. Sounds spooky, until you count: it's not about *your* birthday matching someone — it's about any of the 253 possible pairs matching. Tons of chances, each one small, one of them hits.

So the trick when you spot a pattern is to ask: how many opportunities were there for *some* pattern to show up? In big batches of random data, the answer is usually "a huge number" — and clumps, streaks, and coincidences are exactly what randomness looks like when it's doing its job.
