余数是 5。

推导：7^2=49≡10 (mod 13)，7^3≡10×7=70≡5，7^6≡5×5=25≡12≡−1 (mod 13)，所以 7^12≡(−1)^2≡1。又 123=12×10+3，故 7^123≡7^3≡5 (mod 13)。验证：343=13×26+5。

[A02]
打破杯子的是甲。

理由：逐一检验。
- 若甲打破：甲说“乙打破的”为假；乙说“不是我”为真；丙说“不是甲”为假 → 恰有一句真，成立。
- 若乙打破：甲真、乙假、丙真 → 两句真，不成立。
- 若丙打破：甲假、乙真、丙真 → 两句真，不成立。
所以只能是甲，此时唯一为真的一句是乙的话。

[A03]

[A12]
26 个。

计数方法：首位与末位不同，分两类且由反转对称，故总数 = 2×（首位 1、末位 0 的个数）。
固定首位为 1、末位为 0，中间 6 位（第 2–7 位）须恰有 3 个 1；又因首位是 1，中间最前两位不能同时为 1（否则出现 111），且中间自身不能有 111。
中间 6 位恰 3 个 1 的排法共 C(6,3)=20 种，排除：
- 出现 111 的：{1,2,3}、{2,3,4}、{3,4,5}、{4,5,6} 这 4 种；
- 以 11 开头的（与首位合成 111）：{1,2,4}、{1,2,5}、{1,2,6} 这 3 种（与上一组不重叠）。
故每类 20−4−3=13 种，总数为 2×13=26。

因为平均数只告诉你“重心在哪”，不告诉你“东西摊得有多开”。比如两组人体重都是 60 公斤：第一组是 58、59、60、61、62，第二组是 30、45、60、75、90——平均一样，但第二组里有特别瘦和特别胖的人，你看到的画面完全不同。除了“分散程度”，分布形状也影响印象：一组可能是大多数人都接近平均值，另一组可能是少数极端值把平均拉过去了（比如 9 个人挣 3 千、1 个人挣 100 万，平均也能很好看，但并不代表大家都过得不错）。所以看数据至少要三样：平均数、离散程度（如标准差、极差）、以及分布形状或中位数，必要时直接画个图看。

[B12]
Here's the thing: "random" describes how each step is chosen, not what the whole pile of steps looks like. If I flip a fair coin 100 times, every single sequence of 100 flips is equally unlikely — but I'm not looking at sequences, I'm looking at summaries like "how many heads?" And most summaries are produced by tons of sequences while extreme ones are produced by very few. That's why you'll almost always see something close to 50 heads, and almost never 95. The clumping is the same story: 10 flips only have about a thousand possible outcomes, so streaks like HHHHH happen by chance more often than intuition says. Your brain then does the damage — it's tuned to spot faces in clouds, so a run of six heads looks like "the coin is hot" instead of "one of many equally ordinary patterns." In fact, if a "random" list never showed any streaks, that would be the suspicious part, because truly independent flips should repeat themselves sometimes. So a simulation making patterns isn't proof it's broken; the real test is statistical: run it many times and check that, say, the long-run proportion of heads sits near 50%, and that results at one moment don't predict the next. Patterns in the short run, regularity in the long run — that's what randomness actually looks like.
