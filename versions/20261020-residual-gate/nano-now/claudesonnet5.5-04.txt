乙打破：甲真，乙假，丙真，两真，不符。
丙打破：甲假，乙真，丙真，两真，不符。
所以唯一成立的是甲打破，修正答案：打破杯子的是**甲**。

[B08]
想象两个班级的平均分都是70分。一个班里几乎每个人都考了六七十分，大家水平差不多；另一个班里一半人考了100分，另一半人只考了40分，差距很大。平均数一样，可你对这两个班的感受会完全不同：前者稳定，后者两极分化。
原因是平均数只告诉你"中间大概在哪"，却没告诉你数据有多分散、有没有极端值、分布是否对称。所以看数据时，除了平均数，最好再问一句："大家离这个平均数有多远？"

Here's the surprising thing: a simulation of randomness isn't actually random. A computer uses a formula that starts from a number called a seed and churns out a long sequence that *looks* random. Run it with the same seed and you get exactly the same sequence every time. So patterns can come from the formula itself, especially if it's a weak one.

But even a perfect source of randomness produces patterns, and that's the more interesting part. Our brains expect randomness to look evenly mixed, like H-T-H-T-H-T. Real random coin flips don't behave that way. In 100 flips, you'd expect a streak of about six heads or tails in a row somewhere. If you saw none, that would actually be suspicious.

Think of scattering sprinkles on a cupcake without trying to space them out. Some will clump together and some bare patches will appear, and that's what "no planning" really looks like. Randomness means each outcome doesn't depend on the last one, not that things get neatly spread out. Clumps, streaks, and coincidences are just what chance looks like up close.
