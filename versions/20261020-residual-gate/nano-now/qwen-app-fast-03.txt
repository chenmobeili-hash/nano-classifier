[B12] Because randomness isn’t the same as “evenly spread out.” When you flip a coin many times, each flip is independent, but the collection of flips starts to follow predictable patterns—like getting roughly half heads and half tails. That’s the law of large numbers: individual outcomes are wild, but aggregates become smooth. Simulations also use pseudo-random number generators, which are deterministic algorithms that mimic randomness, so they can accidentally produce repeating patterns. And our brains are pattern-hunting machines: we’ll spot “streaks” or “clusters” even in truly random data, because we’re wired to find meaning. So a simulation can look patterned both because real randomness has statistical regularities, and because the tool generating it isn’t perfectly chaotic.
