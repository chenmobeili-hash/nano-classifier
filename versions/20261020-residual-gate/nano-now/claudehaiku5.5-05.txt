平均数只告诉我们一组数据的“中心”在哪里，却没有说明数据围绕这个中心散布得有多开。举个例子，两个班级的数学平均分都是70分。第一个班大家的成绩都在65到75之间，比较集中；第二个班有人只考了40分，也有人考了100分，两极分化明显。虽然平均分相同，但如果只看平均数，就会误以为两个班的情况差不多，而实际上第二个班的学生差异很大，老师需要关注的重点也完全不同。所以，要理解一组数据，除了看平均数，还要看它的分散程度。

Here's a fun way to think about it. A computer can't really flip a coin, so it uses a formula that spits out numbers that look random but are actually fully determined by a starting value. Even so, the results pass for random because real randomness doesn't look evenly spread out. If you flip a coin 100 times, you'll almost certainly see a streak of five or six heads in a row somewhere, and that streak feels like a pattern even though every flip was independent. People expect randomness to alternate neatly between heads and tails, but true randomness clumps. So when a simulation produces clusters, streaks, or gaps, it's usually doing exactly what randomness should do. The patterns you see are the normal shape of chance over many tries.
