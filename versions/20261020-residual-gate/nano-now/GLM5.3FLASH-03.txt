- r=4（四段各长1）：z₀≥1，中间三个间隔零加末尾零共剩 1 个自由零 → 1 种；
- r=3（2,1,1 的3种排列）：z₀≥1，z₀+两间隔零=2 → 每种排列3种，共 9 种；
- r=2（两段各长2）：z₀≥1，z₀+中间间隔=3 → 3 种。

因为平均数只告诉我们要“整体水准”，却不告诉我们数据是怎么围绕这个水准分布的。举个例子：两个班级平均分都是75分。A班可能所有人的成绩都在70到80之间，大家水平很整齐；B班可能一半人考95分以上、一半人只考50分，高的很高、低的很低。两组一平均都是75，但给人的印象完全不同：一个像“大家都不错”，另一个像“贫富悬殊”。造成这种差异的关键是数据的“波动程度”（统计学里叫方差或标准差），此外极个别特别大或特别小的数值也会把平均数拉偏——比如一个班大部分考60分，只有一个考了200分（假设满分不止100），平均分就会虚高。所以只看平均数，就像只看一个人的身高就猜测他的体型，身高相同的人胖瘦可以差很远；我们还需要看分布、极值和离散程度，才能得到完整的印象。

This is one of my favorite little surprises about randomness: it doesn't look random the way we expect it to. Imagine flipping a coin over and over. Most people assume a "truly random" sequence should alternate a lot — heads, tails, heads, tails — because that feels fair and mixed. But a fair coin has no memory. After three heads in a row, the next flip is still 50/50, so streaks like HHHHT or TTTHH show up all the time. In a run of a hundred flips, a streak of five or six identical results is not just possible, it's practically guaranteed.

There's a second reason patterns appear, and it lives in our heads rather than in the coins. Our brains are extraordinary pattern-detection machines — that's how we learned to spot predators in rustling grass — so when we look at a scatter of random dots and see a cluster, or hear a "hot streak" in a simulation, we're often finding shapes that were never planted there. Show ten people the same random noise and several will find something meaningful in it.

Put those two things together and you get the punchline: a simulation of randomness produces patterns because genuine randomness naturally clumps, drifts, and streaks, and because we're built to notice when it does. Ironically, a sequence that looks perfectly "shuffled and balanced" is often the one a human made up — real randomness is messier than our intuition about it, and that messiness is exactly what randomness should look like.
