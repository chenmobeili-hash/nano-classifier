平均数只告诉你数据的“中心”在哪，却不说数据“散开”的程度。比如两组平均都是50：一组是 49、50、51，另一组是 10、50、90。第一组人人都接近50，很稳定；第二组忽高忽低，看着就“不太对劲”。所以我们感受到的差异其实来自波动大小（离散程度），这是平均数这个单一数字无法传达的信息。

Randomness is surprising to our brains because we expect it to look "evenly scattered" — so when a simulation produces clusters, streaks, or near-symmetric shapes, it feels like a hidden pattern or even a trick. It isn't. True randomness is clumpy by nature: if you flip a coin 100 times, a run of six heads in a row is actually quite likely somewhere in the sequence. Our eyes are pattern-seeking machines, evolved to spot faces and order, so they light up on any structure and whisper "that can't be chance." A simulation also uses deterministic math under the hood — a pseudo-random number generator — so technically every pattern is reproducible, which makes the illusion stronger. The real lesson is that a small sample of randomness often looks ordered, and the disorder we expect only shows up over huge amounts of data. So when a random simulation "makes a picture," it's usually our pattern detector, not the simulation, doing the interesting work.
