理由（P/N 位置分析，P = 轮到走的人必败，规定 0 为 P）：从 0 递推得各位置状态为
0:P，1:N，2:P，3:N，4:N，5:N，6:N，7:P，8:N，9:P，10:N，11:N，12:N，13:N，14:P，15:N。
P 位置集合为 {0, 2, 7, 9, 14}。15 是 N 位置，故先手必胜。三种开局中：取 3 留 12（N）、取 4 留 11（N），均把必胜位置送给对手；只有取 1 留 14（P）正确。此后对手从 14 只能走到 13、11、10（全为 N），先手总能回到 P 位置：13→取 4→9；11→取 4→7；10→取 1→9（或取 3→7）。如此反复把 P 位置留给对手，直至取走最后一枚。

**26** 个。可复核方法（补集 + 间隔法）：
1) 长度 8、恰 4 个 1、无三个连续 1 的字符串总数：排 4 个 0 形成 5 个空隙，每隙放 0~2 个 1、总和为 4。无上限解 C(8,4)=70；某隙 ≥3 的解为 5（个空隙）×C(5,4)=25（两隙同时 ≥3 不可能）。总数 70−25=45。
2) 首位=末位=0：中间 6 位放恰 4 个 1 且无三个连续，即 3 个内部空隙各放 0~2、和为 4：排列 (2,2,0) 共 3 种、(2,1,1) 共 3 种，合计 6。

因为平均数只是把所有数据“摊平”成的一个数，它把数据内部的形状完全抹掉了。举个例子：两个班的平均身高都是 170 厘米。A 班所有人都在 168 到 172 之间，站出来整整齐齐；B 班一半人是 185、一半人是 155，站出来高矮分明。平均数一模一样，但一个班“大家都差不多”，另一个班“差距非常大”，给人的印象完全不同。所以看数据不能只看平均数，还要看数据是聚在一起还是散得很开、有没有个别极端值把平均数拉高或拉低、大部分值落在哪里。平均数回答的是“摊到每个人头上是多少”，却不回答“人和人之间差多远”——而后者往往才是我们真正感受到的东西。

Great question — and honestly, this one trips up plenty of adults too.

Here's the key idea: random doesn't mean "evenly spread out" or "pattern-free." It just means every outcome has some chance, and what happened before doesn't influence what happens next. But when you string together lots of random events, clumps and streaks aren't just possible — they're basically guaranteed.

Flip a coin 80 times and you'll almost certainly see five or six heads in a row somewhere. It feels meaningful — like the coin "got hot" — but it's exactly what chance produces. Weirdly, if you never saw a streak, *that* would be the suspicious outcome.

Two things are going on here. First, small samples are lumpy: with only a handful of events, chance hasn't had enough throws to smooth itself out, so bumps and runs are normal. Second, your brain is a pattern-finding machine. It evolved to spot a tiger hiding in the grass, so it reads structure into noise even when there's nothing there — psychologists call this apophenia. Hand people a page of random dots and they'll swear they see faces and shapes in it.

So when a simulation of randomness shows patterns — streaks, clusters, bands — that's not a bug or evidence of some hidden order. A truly random world is full of little accidents lining up. The patterns are real in the sense that you genuinely see them; they just don't have a cause.
