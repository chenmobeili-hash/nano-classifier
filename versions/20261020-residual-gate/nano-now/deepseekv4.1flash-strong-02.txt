[B08] 平均数只告诉你数据的"重心"在哪，不告诉你数据是怎么摊开的。举个例子：两个班平均分都是 80。甲班所有人都在 76 到 84 之间，你会觉得"这个班水平整齐"；乙班一半人考 60、一半人考 100，你看到的是"两极分化"。平均分完全一样，印象完全相反。再比如九个人月薪五千、一个人月薪五十万，平均月薪被抬到五万多，但绝大多数人并不"平均"。所以看数据还要看：最大和最小差多少（离散程度）、大多数人集中在哪儿、有没有极端值把平均数拖走、分布是单峰还是双峰。平均数回答"中心在哪"，印象来自"大家离中心多远、怎么分布"。

[B12] Think about what randomness actually looks like. Most people picture something like H T H T T H — nicely alternating, evenly mixed. Real randomness doesn't behave that way. It clumps. If you flip a coin 80 times, runs of five or six heads in a row are not a glitch; they're expected, because each flip has no memory of the last one. A streak isn't the coin "heating up" — it's just what independent events do once you have enough of them. With 80 flips there are 76 overlapping windows of five, and each window has a 1-in-32 chance of being all heads, so seeing a long run somewhere is actually likely, not suspicious. The same thing happens with dice: in 24 rolls you'll probably see a number repeat back-to-back, and some numbers will show up four times while others show up twice. That's not the dice cheating; it's the difference between "even in the long run" and "even in a short sample." There's a second reason too: you're a pattern-spotting machine. Your brain is built to notice streaks and coincidences and to forget the thousands of boring, unremarkable stretches in between. So when a simulation spits out HHHHH, that stands out and feels designed, while the ordinary parts of the sequence leave no trace in memory. Put those two together — genuine clumping plus selective attention — and a perfectly honest random simulation can look like it's hiding a message. The test isn't whether a pattern appears; it's whether the pattern appears more often than the maths predicts.
