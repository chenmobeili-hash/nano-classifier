Imagine flipping a coin again and again. Even though each flip is random, you will not get a perfectly even mix of heads and tails in short stretches. Sometimes you get five heads in a row, and your brain wants to say "something is going on here." That is the trick: random processes produce clumps, streaks, and gaps by chance alone. Simulations work the same way. When a computer generates lots of random numbers, the outcomes naturally bunch up in certain shapes, such as the bell curve. Patterns show up not because someone designed them, but because chance, repeated many times, tends to create structure. Once you see that, randomness stops looking like chaos and starts looking like a source of surprising order.
