**甲打破了杯子。** 逐一假设：若乙打破，则甲真（乙打破的）、丙真（不是甲）→ 两句真，不符；若丙打破，则乙真、丙真 → 两句真，不符；若甲打破，则甲假、乙假（确是“我”打破，即“不是我”为假）、丙真（确实不是甲？不对——是甲打破时“不是甲”为假）——重新核对：甲打破时，甲的话“乙打破的”为假；乙的话“不是我打破的”为真？乙没打破，所以“不是我”为**真**；丙的话“不是甲打破的”为假。恰好一句真（乙的话），符合。故唯一自洽解：甲打破。等等——更正前面：甲打破时只有乙说真话，恰一句真，成立；乙打破时甲真且丙真（两句真），丙打破时乙真且丙真（两句真）。所以只有"甲打破"满足，结论：**甲**。

因为平均数只告诉你数据的"中心"，却不告诉你数据的"散布程度"。想象两个班数学平均分都是75分：A班全班都考70–80分，B班一半人考100分、一半人考50分。平均数一样，但A班给你"整齐稳定"的印象，B班给你"两极分化"的印象。造成这种差别的是**方差**（或标准差），它衡量数据离平均数有多远。此外，**偏态**也会骗人：比如公司里老板月薪极高，会把员工平均工资拉得很高，让人觉得"大家工资都不错"，实际多数人远低于平均数。所以只看平均数就像只看一个人的身高就判断他的体型——你还需要知道"胖瘦"（离散度）和"分布形状"（偏态、峰度）才能形成可靠印象。这也是为什么新闻里说"人均收入"时，学会统计的人会追问：中位数是多少？差距有多大？

Why randomness "shows patterns": here's the honest answer. A simulation of randomness is just a machine that, in a sense, flips coins at a phenomenal rate. But your brain didn't evolve to see "randomness" — it evolved to see faces, tigers, and connections between events. So when you look at a run of random coin flips and notice "HTHTHTHT," something in you stirs: you spot *structure*. And you're not wrong, in a way — short runs of pure chance really do produce things that look like streaks, clusters, and near-regular rhythm. That's the key insight: patterns are cheap, and pure chance is surprisingly good at producing them.

There's even a famous thought experiment: generate 200 random coin flips, and ask people to fake another 200 without a computer. The forgeries almost always *lack* the long streaks and the surprising repetitions that true randomness naturally delivers. Real randomness is messier, clumpier, and more "patterned-looking" than people expect. So when a simulation "looks patterned," it's usually not a bug — it's a faithful snapshot of what unbiased disorder actually feels like from up close.

The deeper lesson is about expectation: with any random process, try generating the same data many times and compare. When the pattern shows up *again* across independent runs, you're seeing a structural feature of the simulation itself. When it shows up once and never again, it was just noise wearing a costume. Learning to tell those apart is one of the most useful — and most humbling — skills a curious mind can pick up.
