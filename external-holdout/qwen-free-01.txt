[A02] 丙打破了杯子。理由：若甲真，则乙打破，此时乙的话“不是我打破的”为假，丙的话“不是甲打破的”为真，出现两句真话，矛盾。若乙真，则甲假（不是乙），丙若真则不是甲，无人打破，矛盾；若丙假则是甲打破，此时甲假乙真丙假，但甲说“乙打破的”为假成立，乙说“不是我打破的”为真成立，丙说“不是甲打破的”为假成立，恰一真，但此时是甲打破。等等，重新检验：设打破者为X。甲说X=乙，乙说X≠乙，丙说X≠甲。三句恰一真。若X=甲：甲假，乙真（X≠乙），丙假（X=甲所以“不是甲”为假）→ 一真，符合。若X=乙：甲真，乙假，丙真（X≠甲）→ 两真，不符。若X=丙：甲假，乙真，丙真 → 两真，不符。故打破者是甲。

[B12] When you flip a coin a few times, the results look messy and unpredictable. But if you flip it thousands of times, something interesting happens: the proportion of heads settles close to 50%. That's because randomness at the individual level follows rules at the group level. Each flip is independent, but the law of large numbers guarantees that over many trials, the average outcome converges to the expected value. Simulations make this visible — a single random walk looks jagged, but run a hundred of them and you'll see a fuzzy cone shape emerge. The pattern isn't in any one outcome; it's in the collective behavior of many outcomes. That's why casinos always win in the long run, even though any single bet is uncertain.
