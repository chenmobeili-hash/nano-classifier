  是绝大多数人收入接近平均，另一座是被几个富翁拉高的平均。人的印象主要来自数据的分布和波动（方差），而不只是

  If you flip ten coins and write down the results, you might see something like HHTTHHTTTH — and your brain
  immediately says "hey, look, a pattern!" But here's the thing: almost any sequence of coin flips looks
  patterned if you stare at it long enough. You'll find runs, echoes, clusters — because in a short sequence,
   some clustering is practically guaranteed. True randomness doesn't look smoothly mixed like salt and
  pepper; it lumps. In fact, a perfectly alternating HTHTHTHT is the rarest-feeling outcome of all, and if it
   showed up you'd rightly suspect a trick. The nice part is you don't have to take my word for it — generate
   a few dozen sequences yourself (or with a friend) and compare. The "patterns" you find will look different
   every time, never repeating, and that's the real signature of randomness: not the absence of pattern, but
  the absence of a plan behind the pattern.
