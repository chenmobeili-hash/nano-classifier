  均数更能说明问题。

  [B12]
  Imagine you ask a computer to "flip a coin" a thousand times. It isn't really flipping anything. It runs a recipe, a
  formula that turns one number into the next, and the results are designed to look random. That's called pseudo-
  randomness.

  Even so, patterns can show up, for two reasons.

  1. Real randomness is clumpy. If you flip a fair coin 20 times, getting 5 heads in a row somewhere isn't weird. It's
  actually pretty likely. Our brains expect randomness to look evenly mixed, like HTHTHT, but that's too neat. Truly
  random data has streaks and clusters, and our pattern-spotting brains notice them.

  2. A simulation has a hidden recipe. Because the formula is fixed, a poor generator can repeat itself after a while
  or produce numbers that line up in odd ways. Good generators make this extremely hard to notice, but the rule is
  still there underneath.

  So a pattern doesn't automatically mean something is broken. A random-looking result with no streaks at all would be
  the more suspicious one.
