[B08] 平均数只告诉你数据的"重心"在哪里，却不告诉你数据是怎么散开、怎么分布的。两组平均身高一样，一组是大家都差不多（比如170±2厘米），另一组是有人极高有人很矮（同样平均170）。后者给人的印象截然不同：风险更大、特例更多、多数人其实不在平均线上。日常感受依赖的是"典型个体长什么样、波动有多大"，也就是分布形状和离散程度，而不只是一个汇总数字。所以说，平均数是必要信息，但单独看它会掩盖很多故事。

[B12] A simulation of randomness can produce patterns because "random" doesn't mean "pattern-free" — it means "not designed." When you flip a coin many times, you'll inevitably get stretches of the same result, clusters, even runs that look surprisingly tidy. That's not the simulation cheating; it's just how chance behaves when you look closely. Humans are also superb pattern-detectors — we evolved to notice shapes, trends, and coincidences — so our eyes pick structure out of noise even when none was intended. Imagine dropping sand onto a table one grain at a time: each grain lands unpredictably, yet a pile with slopes and ridges emerges. In the same way, random events, once accumulated, settle into visible textures — streaks, waves, clusters. The deep point is that patterns and randomness live at different layers: each individual event is unpredictable, but collections of events have statistics, and those statistics show up to our eyes as patterns. A good simulation isn't failing when you spot one; you're simply seeingChance wearing a pattern as a costume.
