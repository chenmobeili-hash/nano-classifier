计数方法：首位≠末位分两种情形 (s₁=0,s₈=1) 和 (s₁=1,s₈=0)，由串的左右反转变换一一对应，两种情形数目相等，设为 X，答案=2X。

平均数只告诉你“总的说，中等水平在哪”，却不告诉你数据是怎么散开的。举个例子：两个宿舍平均分都是75分。甲宿舍全班都在70–80之间，人人差不多；乙宿舍一半人考95分、一半人考55分，平均也是75。数字上完全一样，但印象完全不同：一个像整齐的队伍，一个像两极分化。关键在于“波动大小”（在统计里叫离散程度）：平均数把高低相互抵消了，抵消之后，这些高低差异本身就不见了。所以同样的平均数背后，可能藏着稳定性与波动、均衡与悬殊的巨大差别——只看平均数，就像只听一首歌的和弦却听不见节奏，很多东西被抹平了。

Here's the fun secret: randomness is really good at accidentally looking like a plan. If you flip a coin ten times, a run of five heads in a row isn't a message from the universe — it's just one of the many shapes ten flips can take, and somebody has to win the "all shapes" lottery. With enough chances, the unlikely ones show up.

Think of it like rain on a window. Each drop lands wherever physics and chance say, no pattern intended — yet you'll see streaks, gaps, little clusters that look almost designed. Your brain, which evolved to find faces in clouds and danger in shadows, can't help reading stories into them. That's not a bug in the coin or the rain; it's the way patterns are distributed: randomly, but not uniformly smoothly.

There's also a mathematical reason. In a truly random sequence, some structures *must* appear — runs, near-symmetries, clusters. So when you spot one, you've found exactly the thing randomness was guaranteed to produce somewhere. The trick a good simulation teaches you is humility: before you say "look, a pattern!", ask how likely that pattern is under pure chance. If the answer is "pretty likely, given enough tries," then the pattern is just chance wearing a costume. Learning to tell costumes apart from real causes is honestly one of the most useful superpowers a curious teenager can pick up.
