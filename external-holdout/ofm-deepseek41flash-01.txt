[B08]
平均数只告诉你“总量摊平后每份多少”，却完全没告诉你数据散不散、怎么分布。比如两个班平均分都是75：甲班大多在70到80之间，人人中等；乙班一半人95分、一半人55分，平均也是75。数字一样，但甲班“整齐”，乙班“两极分化”，给人的感觉天差地别。另外还要看极端值：一个团队里10个人年薪都是10万，平均10万；若9个人9万、老板100万，平均就变成18万多——这个“平均”已经不能代表任何人。所以看平均数时，最好再问一句：数据波动多大？有没有离群值？分布是集中还是分散？这就是标准差、分位数这些指标存在的意义。

Because a simulator only promises you the *odds*, not a tidy-looking result. Flip a fair coin 20 times and you might get HHHHHTTTTTHHHHHTTTTT — that's 4 runs, when "random-looking" intuition expects constant alternation. Surprising streaks aren't a bug; they're what independence looks like. Each flip ignores the last one, so long runs happen at exactly the rate probability predicts.

There are a few other reasons patterns jump out. Small samples are noisy: in 10 rolls of a die, seeing three 6s feels meaningful, but it's totally ordinary. Coincidences multiply: you notice the one time your "random" numbers formed 1234 and ignore the thousands of boring ones. And our brains are pattern detectors on overdrive — we see faces in clouds, so we see "design" in noise.

Here's the fun part: patterns and predictability are different things. Over many trials, aggregate behaviour becomes extremely predictable — about half heads, about 1/6 sixes, and the proportions tighten as the count grows (that's the law of large numbers). So randomness is messy locally and orderly globally. A simulation showing clumps, streaks, and lopsided patches is usually behaving *more* correctly than one that looks perfectly even — a too-smooth sequence is often the sign of a bad generator, or of a human pretending to be random. 🐳
